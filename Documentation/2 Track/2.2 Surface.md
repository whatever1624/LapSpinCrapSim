# Concept

The surface is in $sn$ curvilinear coordinates, where:
- $s$ -Longitudinal distance along the surface spine (positive forwards)
	- ==ACTUALLY CHANGE THIS TO `u`, SO THAT `s` CAN BE RESERVED FOR ACTUAL DISPLACEMENTS==
- $n$ - Lateral distance from the surface spine (positive left)
- $\theta$ - Angle of the surface spine (anti-clockwise from the $x$ axis)

A surface spine maps the $sn$ coordinates to the $xy$ coordinates

A polynomial fit across the track width maps the $sn$ curvilinear coordinates to the $z$ coordinate

## Surface Spine

The surface spine is a 2D spline representing the minimum-curvature trajectory between the soft track limits, and is parameterised in $s$

The lateral position is parameterised in $n$ normal to the surface spine trajectory, where $n=0$ for all points on the surface spine

Track limits are enforced by bounding the valid $n$ coordinates at each $s$ coordinate

*Note: Minimum-curvature trajectory used so that each "mesh cell" $ds$ by $dn$ is more square and more similar in size, which should avoid "pinch points" on the inside of tight corners where a small distance on track is a large change in $s$, which can result in excessive $z$ gradients for a 3D track*

*Note: Since the minimum-curvature trajectory of the surface spine doesn't account for vehicle width, the legal $n$ coordinate for track limits at certain points may not include $n=0$ if the hard track limits are close to the soft track limits*

## Surface Polynomial Fit

The 3D nature of the surface is encoded by a polynomial fit of the lateral coordinate $n$ to the $z$ coordinates of the track data
$$z=p_0+p_1n+p_2n^2+...+p_Nn^N$$
Where $N$ is the degree of the polynomial fit, which must be less than the number of strips of $z$ coordinates provided for the track data

The polynomial fit is represented as an $(N+1)$-dimensional spline parameterised in $s$, where each dimension represents the corresponding polynomial coefficient

The surface normal in the $xyz$ coordinates is computed as the surface normal in $snz$ coordinates ($\vec n_{uvz}={\partial z\over\partial u}\times{\partial z\over\partial v}$) multiplied by the transformation matrix to $xyz$ coordinates
$$
\vec{unscaled\_normal}_{xyz}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
{\partial x\over\partial s}&{\partial x\over\partial n}&0\\
{\partial y\over\partial s}&{\partial y\over\partial n}&0\\
0&0&1\\
\end{bmatrix}
\Big({\partial z\over\partial s}\times{\partial z\over\partial n}\Big)
$$
Then surface normal is scaled to unit length

		- Minimum-curvature trajectory used so that each "mesh cell" $ds$ by $dn$ is more square and more similar in size, which should avoid "pinch points" on the inside of tight corners where a small distance on track is a large change in $s$, which can result in excessive $z$ gradients for a 3D track
		- Note the minimum-curvature trajectory of the surface spine assumes 0 vehicle width
	- Lateral position parameterised in $n$ - this position is normal to the spine trajectory
		- $n=0$ for all points on the surface spine
		- Track limits enforced by bounding the valid $n$ coordinates at each $s$ coordinate
		- Note that since the minimum curvature trajectory of the surface spine doesn't account for vehicle width, the legal $n$ coordinate for track limits at certain points (entry/apex/exit) may not include $n=0$ if the hard track limits are close to the soft track limits
	- Derivatives ($x$ and $y$ with respect to $s$ and $n$) computed from the spline methods
		- Python: `derivative()`
		- Modelica: `Modelica.Blocks.Tables.Internal.getDerTable1DValue()`

	- Polynomial fit of the lateral coordinate $n$ to the $z$ coordinates of the track data
		- $z=p_0+p_1n+p_2n^2+...+p_Nn^N$
		- Note that the degree of the polynomial fit must be less than the number of strips of $z$ coordinates provided for the track data
	- Represented as an $(N+1)$-dimensional spline (where $N$ is the polynomial degree) parameterised in $s$, where each dimension represents the corresponding polynomial coefficeint
	- Derivatives
		- In the longitudinal $s$ direction ($\partial z\over\partial s$) computed as
			- ${\partial z\over\partial u}={\partial p_0\over\partial u}+{\partial p_0\over\partial u}v+{\partial p_0\over\partial u}v^2+...+{\partial p_0\over\partial u}v^n$
			- Using the spline methods
				- Python: `derivative()`
				- Modelica: `Modelica.Blocks.Tables.Internal.getDerTable1DValue()`
		- In the lateral $n$ direction (${\partial z\over\partial n}$) computed as
			- ${\partial z\over\partial n}=p_1+2p_2v+...+np_nv^{n-1}$
	- Surface normal in $xyz$ coordinates computed as
		- The surface normal in $snz$ coordinates ($\vec n_{uvz}={\partial z\over\partial u}\times{\partial z\over\partial v}$) multiplied by the transformation matrix to $xyz$ coordinates, then scaled to unit length
		- $\vec{unscaled\_normal}_{xyz}=\begin{bmatrix}{\partial x\over\partial s}&{\partial x\over\partial n}&0\\{\partial y\over\partial s}&{\partial y\over\partial n}&0\\0&0&1\\\end{bmatrix}\Big({\partial z\over\partial s}\times{\partial z\over\partial n}\Big)$
